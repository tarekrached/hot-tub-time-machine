# Bromine 3-Step Method

> Source: [How to use Bromine (3-step method) — Pool Spa Forum](https://www.poolspaforum.com/forum/index.php?/topic/53410-how-to-use-bromine-3-step-method/)

## Test Kit

When testing water do NOT use strips, get a good drop based test kit. Your best bet for Bromine if you are in the US or Canada is the **Taylor K-2106** and for chlorine the K-2006 (NOT the K-2005). The test kit is probably the most important thing you can own for easy spa maintenance.

If the K-2106 is not readily available in Canada you can use the K-2006 for bromine by testing for Free Chlorine and multiplying the results by 2.25 to get Total Bromine. You do not need to test for combined bromine (combined chlorine test) nor cyanuric acid in a bromine system.

---

## Step 1: Fill and Balance the Water

Fill the spa and balance the water. Do not turn the heat up yet. If you have well water or know your water has metals then add a metal sequestrant at this point.

### A. Adjust Total Alkalinity (TA)

- Target: **50–70 ppm**
- Raise TA with **baking soda** if too low (same chemical sold as "TA increaser" for much less money)
- Lower TA by following a detailed TA-lowering procedure if too high

### B. Adjust pH

- Target: **7.4–7.8**
- Lower pH with **dry acid** (or muriatic acid) if too high (8.0 and above)
- If pH is too low (7.2 and below), either **aerate** the water to outgas CO2 and bring up the pH, or use **borax** from the laundry aisle to raise it
- Do NOT use "pH up" (sodium carbonate / washing soda) — it raises both pH AND TA

### C. Adjust Calcium Hardness (CH)

- If below 130 ppm: raise to **130–150 ppm**
- If above 400 ppm: add anti-stain and scale or calcium reducer weekly
- If between 130–400 ppm: you are fine

---

## Step 2: Add Sodium Bromide (Bromide Bank)

Add **1/2 oz of sodium bromide per 100 gallons** of water to create the bromide reserve. This is the MOST important step with a bromine spa and the one most people ignore. If you omit this step you will not have a bromine spa for several weeks until enough tablets dissolve in the water to create the bromide bank.

- Get sodium bromide in either powder or liquid form
- READ THE LABEL — it should only contain sodium bromide (and water if in liquid form)
- Re-add on each drain and fill

> **Canada note:** Sodium bromide has been outlawed in Canada except as part of "one step" bromine sanitizer products (mixture of sodium bromide and dichlor). A workaround is to use such a product for the first few weeks to build the bromide bank.

---

## Step 3: Shock to Activate

Shock the spa to "activate" (oxidize) the sodium bromide into hypobromous acid (this is the "bromine" sanitizer).

### Using bleach (sodium hypochlorite)

Use regular, unscented liquid chlorine bleach:

| Bleach Concentration | Amount per 100 gallons |
|---|---|
| 5.25% | 3 oz (6 tablespoons) |
| 6% | 2.5 oz (5 tablespoons) |

Bromine will go very high. Uncover the spa and circulate until the bromine drops below 10 ppm before using the spa. Now heat the spa up to temperature.

---

## Ongoing Maintenance: Bromine Tablets

Put in the floater with bromine tabs (which usually contain BOTH bromine and chlorine to activate the bromine) and adjust the floater to maintain bromine at about **4–6 ppm**.

### Before each use

- Check pH and bromine
- If bromine is low, add a few tablespoons of bleach and retest until above 4 but below 10 ppm
- If pH is not between 7.2 and 8.0, adjust it before entering and wait about 30 minutes then retest
- If both are off, adjust pH first then bromine

> **Important:** You cannot accurately test pH when sanitizer levels are high due to an interaction between high sanitizer levels and the phenol red indicator. Most test kits/strips are inaccurate above 3–5 ppm sanitizer. Taylor kits are good up to 10 ppm. The only workaround is a properly calibrated pH meter.

### Floater adjustment

- Bromine always low → open the floater more
- Bromine always high → close it down
- Above 10 ppm → remove the floater, open the spa until bromine drops below 10, then close the floater down a bit
- Keep tablets in the floater at all times

---

## Maintenance Schedule

| Task | Frequency | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Check pH & bromine | Before each use | Adjust as needed |
| Shock with bleach | Weekly | Same amount as initial shock (no additional sodium bromide needed) |
| Check & adjust TA and calcium | Every 2–4 weeks | Do before adjusting pH. Every 2 weeks initially until you learn your tub, then monthly if stable |
| Drain, refill, rebalance | Every 3–4 months | Full process: balance water, add sodium bromide, shock, replace bromine tablets |

---

## Optional: Borate

Borate has several benefits when added to a **30–50 ppm** range:

- Add with a commercial borate product (Gentle Spa, Optimizer Plus) or using **boric acid** or **borax**
- Boric acid will slightly lower pH (good since spa pH tends to rise)
- Borax will raise pH significantly and require acid to maintain proper range
- Recommendation: boric acid or a commercial pH-neutral product
- Test with LaMotte or Industrial Test Systems borate test strips (easier to read than Taylor/Hach/AquaCheck)
- Initially adjust to 50 ppm, test as often as TA and calcium
- When it drops to 30 ppm, bring it back up to 50 ppm

---

## Bromine vs. Chlorine

Chlorine is cheaper but requires daily attention. Bromine (3-step method) is:

- More forgiving (wider acceptable pH range)
- Easier water balance than chlorine or 2-step bromine
- Only needs attention once or twice a week after initial setup (besides checking sanitizer and pH before each use)
